Water now · FloodDash How Bangkok's water is moving
  • Line width — how far the waterway runs: the Chao Phraya, then the long khlongs like Saen Saep and Prem Prachakon, then local canals.
  • Moving dashes — the normal drainage direction. Tidal reaches and pumped canals can run the other way.
  • Colour — what the nearest gauge says, carried 1.5 km along the same waterway (straight-line). Blue means no gauge concern there, or no gauge at all.

          Who

          Dr Non — Bangkok, 1981 →

          That’s me. Days old. Siam Square — the most expensive ground in Bangkok today. Back then it was just where our house was. I grew up in the heart of this city and learned it from the inside: heat, floods, skytrain openings, AQI at 200, tax schedules frozen while the market doubled.

          I am not going to let anyone fuck up this city. Not the current governors. Not the deputies. Not the committees writing ten-year “smart city” PDFs while the public gets nothing they can use. No apologies. I’m building something — a working twin of the city I was born into. My city. Not theirs.

          Thesis

          One laptop. Zero politicians who care.

          The BKKx Atlas is a thesis statement: a working digital twin of a real city can be built in an afternoon, on one laptop, for the cost of a domain. The data, APIs, and maps are here. What’s missing is a city government with the will to use them.

          BMA has been “planning a smart city” since 2016. Ten years. Ten digital strategies. Zero public APIs worth using, zero digital twin for residents. Buildings get taller. Floods get deeper. The public still gets a 1990s PDF. We shipped the whole stack on a one-laptop budget. The hard part is keeping every layer honest and stackable.

          How it’s built

          Basic infrastructure — still one machine

          • Front. Static MapLibre GL atlas in public/ — no SPA framework. Layers, presets, inspect, and this About drawer are plain HTML / CSS / JS.
          • Edge. Cloudflare Worker (src/index.ts) on atlas.nonarkara.org: proxies live feeds, serves risk / civic APIs, Range-passthrough for PMTiles from R2.
          • Tiles & store. 432,077 OSM buildings as PMTiles on R2; civic amenities in D1 (CIVIC_DB) ingested from BMA / data.go.th.
          • Live inputs. iTIC/Longdo traffic + CCTV, RainViewer radar, Open-Meteo wind/heat/AQI, water gauges judged by FloodDash — honest about what is live vs schematic.
          • Reuse. GeoJSON downloads and /api for Chula, UNL, or anyone building on Bangkok without a consulting fee.

          What’s on the map

          Shipped — stackable, honest

          1. Multi-layer city stack. Buildings under translucent rain, wind, heat, AQI, and flood. Presets: City · Live pulse · Rain now · Flood watch · Satellite air.
          2. Inspect + risk API. Click a building → OSM height/type + schematic flood × live PM2.5 via /api/risk.
          3. Live traffic + CCTV. iTIC/Longdo expressways and public road cameras (HLS). Not the closed BMA security net. /api/cctv
          4. Rail + water transit. Nine rail lines plus four dotted boat corridors and 16 passenger piers. Dots mean scheduled orientation, not pretend-live boats. Ask the multimodal API.
          5. Civic open data. Amenities + density by district from BMA / data.go.th via D1 — /api/civic-pois.
          6. Land price by district. Real Treasury road-level analysis as a choropleth — not a fake heat paint.
          7. Waterways, gauges + rain radar. Every canal sized by reach and flowing downstream, coloured where a FloodDash-judged gauge is worried; animated RainViewer frames over the silhouette. Method on the Research tab.
          8. Current wind field. A 6 × 6 Open-Meteo model grid turns 10 m speed and direction into clickable arrow shafts and heads. It explains where rain and haze may move without pretending to be a street-level sensor network.
          9. Night lights / blackout. Desaturated satellite + emissive buildings — pure client paint.

          The question

          A diagram nobody could read

          The BMA Drainage and Sewerage Department publishes a water-management schematic of the city — แผนผังบริหารจัดการน้ำ กรุงเทพมหานคร — at weather.bangkok.go.th/KlongMap. It is the picture that gets pointed at in flood briefings. It is also a diagram: canals as straight strokes, no ground under them, no scale, nothing that says which way the water drains or where it is high right now.

          The atlas already had the city in 3D over satellite imagery and 4,063 waterway segments from OpenStreetMap. The question for the water layer was plain: can one map show where Bangkok's water runs, which way it drains, and where a gauge says it is high — without claiming more than the data knows?

          FloodDash

          What it is, what it is not

          FloodDash is the atlas's twin for water: a real-time, openly sourced flood watch for every Thai province, run under the Smart City Thailand Office (depa). It streams about 17 public pipelines — HII, RID, PCD, NOAA, Copernicus, Open-Meteo, NASA GPM, GISTDA, TMD, iTIC, CheckFlood CCTV, news — judges every gauge into a band, and every province into safe, watch, prepare or danger. Watch-score weights: water 40 · rain 25 · forecast 15 · wetness 10 · rise 10.

          Its own definition, word for word:

          Useful as a 24-hour-ahead watch and a municipal action board. Not a flood clock.

          We cannot say “this village floods at 14:32.” No DEM/HAND. No street inundation. No village ETA. Silence is not all-clear. No nationwide EVACUATE from a heuristic. Orders stay with ปภ. / อปท. 1784.

          When the data is thin, the useful sentence is the refusal. Do not inflate the claim.

          The atlas inherits that character. A red canal means a gauge on it is worried — often rightly, sometimes not. It never means the street beside it is under water.

          How the atlas reads it

          A relay, not a second opinion

          • Pull. The Worker's cron reads FloodDash's keyless /api/twin, /api/snapshot and /api/dds/briefing about every 10 minutes. Visitors never touch FloodDash; they read the copy in D1.
          • Keep. Each of the three reads fails on its own and keeps its last good copy. A failed refresh never blanks the map, and the age of what you see is always printed.
          • Pass through. Band names, actions and the Bangkok verdict are FloodDash's own words, in English and Thai. The atlas adds placement and age. It never adds a verdict.
          • Why. On 25 Sep 2026, with Bangkok at danger, ThaiWater answered the atlas's shared Cloudflare egress with HTTP 429 and the old gauge layer went blank. FloodDash already ingests that feed once, politely, from one machine.
          • The rule it keeps. A fresh FloodDash reading, or the last good one with its age. Never blank while a copy exists, never an unlabelled age, never a verdict FloodDash did not give. scripts/check-flooddash.ts asserts this against live FloodDash.
          • Open. /api/flood/bkk · /api/flood-sensors · the live block in /api/risk.

          Method

          Width, motion, colour

          1. Width is reach. OSM's width tag here is a type default, not a measurement. So every segment takes the rank of its named waterway by total length across the metro: the Chao Phraya (102 segments, 108 km); 20 km and longer (124 segments — Saen Saep 47 km, Prem Prachakon 40, Samrong 40); 7–20 km (387); other named khlongs (1,672); unnamed ditches (1,778, drawn only close in). 819 named waterways, about 4,000 km in all.
          2. Motion is drawing direction. OSM draws waterways in the direction they flow, and the dashes slide that way. 95 of 102 Chao Phraya segments run southward end to end; the other seven sit on meanders. Tidal reaches and pumped canals can run backwards, so the dashes show nominal drainage, not a measured current. Still, for reduced motion.
          3. Colour is the nearest judged gauge. Each gauge snaps to its own named waterway within 1 km, or to any waterway within 300 m. Segments of that waterway within 1.5 km, straight-line, take its band; the most severe gauge wins; judged stretches swell. Silent and untrusted gauges colour nothing. Blue means no gauge concern — or no gauge at all.

          Limits

          What this map cannot tell you

          • Not inundation. A coloured canal says a gauge on it is near or over its bank. It does not say which streets are wet.
          • Not hydraulic. The 1.5 km reach is a display rule, measured in a straight line — not a flow model.
          • Thin coverage. A few dozen gauges watch about 4,000 km of waterway. Most canals have none; blue there means unknown, not safe.
          • Roads without places. BMA DDS road reports carry no coordinates, so they stay a list by district. “Clearance not recorded” is not “still flooded”.
          • May, never will. No ETA the engine does not trust. No data is not an all-clear.
          • Not a warning. DDPM, BMA and TMD issue warnings. DDPM hotline 1784.

          Still building

          Open questions

          1. Hydraulic reach. Replace the straight-line 1.5 km with distance along the connected network, weighted downstream.
          2. Gates and pumps. DDS publishes gate levels as a bulletin with no coordinates. Placing them needs a source, not a guess.
          3. Water on land. GISTDA's satellite flood extent as the first layer that shows water outside the channels.
          4. Check the colours against the ground. Compare judged stretches with DDS road reports, district by district, across the season.
          5. Official extents. Retire the schematic envelope the day DDPM or BMA publish theirs as open GeoJSON.
