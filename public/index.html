Wildest ideas we can ship

Built by a Bangkokian born in 1981 — more than 30 years inside this city. The data is here. The maps are here. The ideas are below. My city, not theirs.

Research paper 432,077 buildings, 1 system, 0 politicians who care →
  1. 1. BKKx Digital Twin — handover to BMA. Open-source, no lock-in, no ฿5.4M consulting fee. The system proves the technical path; the political handover is the deliverable. BMA gets it for free if they promise to maintain it.
  2. 2. Property-tech API. 432K buildings + heights + 477 land-price zones, served as JSON / GeoJSON / MVT to REITs, developers, banks, insurance underwriters. ฿0.50 per lookup. The data is already on the bucket — just wrap it.
  3. 3. Bangkok Flood Resilience Sandbox. Terrain DEM + rain radar + building heights. Drop a 1-in-100-year storm on the map, watch which blocks flood. Citizens, planners, insurers — same model.
  4. 4. Property Tax Verifier (ภาษีที่ดิน). Every parcel + Treasury assessed value. Property owners enter their deed number, see the official price, compare against their tax bill. Surfaces the gap between what's assessed and what's collected. Civic anti-corruption tool that pays for itself in week one.
  5. 5. Bangkok in the Metaverse. Faithful 1:1 twin of the city, with the same 432K buildings, sell premium virtual plots in Sukhumvit / Silom / Phrom Phuk. ฿50k per virtual address, real estate brokers already asking.
  6. 6. Tourist AR — point, learn. Rattanakosin detail PMTiles + 1,727 temple tags + 75 museums + 498 tombs = a PWA that turns every shrine into a story. No app store. Bangkokians and tourists use the same one.
  7. 7. Urban Planning Decision Support. Drag a new BTS station onto the map, watch land prices around it change. Drag a flood barrier, watch AQI drop. The whole system becomes a sandbox for the decisions BMA should be making.
  8. 8. Insurance Risk API. Premium = parcel × assessed value × flood risk × AQI × building height. One REST call returns the score. ฿2 per lookup, sell to every insurer in Thailand.
  9. 9. BMA Command Center. Live ops dashboard combining every layer this atlas has. A deputy governor walks in, sees the city as a single screen. They don't know how to read it, but at least the data is there. BKKx trains the operators.
  10. 10. Open Data Marketplace. Anonymized foot-traffic patterns derived from pmtiles + tourism data + land-price changes. Sell to retail chains, FMCG brands, urban economists. The "anonymous" promise is real because the underlying PMTiles never tracked people in the first place.

BMA — the Bangkok Metropolitan Administration — has been "planning a smart city" since 2016. Ten years. Ten digital strategies. Zero public APIs, zero open data, zero digital twin. Meanwhile the buildings are getting taller, the floods are getting deeper, and the only dashboard the public gets is a 1990s PDF map. We built the whole thing on a one-laptop budget in an afternoon. The system is not the hard part.
